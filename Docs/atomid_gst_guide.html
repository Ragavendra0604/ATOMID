Start here

The short version, for anyone who reads nothing else.

There is exactly one place in AtomID where tax is calculated. The billing screen, the printed invoice, the thermal receipt, the supplier purchase entry and every GST report all ask the same calculator for their numbers. Nothing computes tax on its own and nothing rounds a figure twice. That is why a total on screen is always the total on the printout.

The calculator needs three answers before it can work: who is selling (your shop's state), what is being sold (each product's tax treatment and rate), and where the sale takes place (the place of supply). If any of the three is missing, the app stops and says so rather than guessing — a guessed tax rate is a wrong invoice you would only discover at filing time.

The rule behind every decision

When the app cannot be certain, it refuses to bill instead of assuming. Every refusal names what is missing and where to fix it.

Three things to set up

Do these once. Everything after that is automatic.

1. Your shop's state

Settings › Business Details. The state you enter here decides whether a sale is inside your state or outside it — which in turn decides whether the customer is charged CGST + SGST or IGST. Without it, nothing can be billed.

2. Each product's tax treatment and rate

On the product form. Every product carries its own treatment (taxable, nil rated, exempt, non-GST) and, if taxable, its own GST rate and cess rate. A product left unconfigured cannot be sold until you set it — the app will not treat "not set" as "0%".

3. Whether your prices include tax

Settings › one choice for the whole shop: prices on the shelf either already contain GST (inclusive) or GST is added at the counter (exclusive). Most Indian retail shops are inclusive, which is the default.

Worth checking with your accountant

Your GST registration status (Regular or Composition) sits in Business Details too. A composition dealer must not collect GST, and the app prints a Bill of Supply instead of a Tax Invoice when the shop is not registered as regular and no tax was charged.

What each setting on a product actually does to the bill.

Nil rated, exempt and non-GST look the same on the bill — no tax — but they are different things in your returns, which is why the app keeps them apart and prints the treatment against each line.

How a bill is worked out

The same six steps, in the same order, every single time. We will follow one real basket all the way through.

The basket we will follow
2 × Cotton shirt @ ₹1,050.00 — GST 5%2,100.00
1 × Denim jeans @ ₹1,120.00 — GST 12%1,120.00
Shop in Tamil Nadu · walk-in customer · prices include tax · 10% discount

Check the shop and find the customer's state

The app reads your shop state, and the customer's state if there is one. If the customer has a GSTIN, the first two digits of that number are the state code, so the app reads them straight from the number.

If the customer's recorded state and their GSTIN disagree, billing stops and names both values. The app never picks a side for you.

Decide the place of supply

For our basket there is no customer record, so the walk-in rule applies and the place of supply is the shop's own state, Tamil Nadu. Seller state and place of supply match, so this is an intra-state sale: CGST + SGST. The full order of preference is here.

Confirm every product's rate

Each line's rate is looked up against the rate book for the date of the bill, not for today. Anything unconfigured, or a rate that was not in force on that date, stops the bill here.

Add up, then spread the discount

Lines are added to a subtotal, then any discount is shared across the lines in proportion to their value — because a discount reduces the taxable value of each line, not just the total at the bottom.

Step 4 — discount allocation

Discount 10%−322.00
Shirts share   322 × 2,100 ÷ 3,220−210.00
Jeans share   322 × 1,120 ÷ 3,220−112.00

Work out the tax, line by line

Because prices include tax, each line's discounted value is divided by 1 + rate to find the value before tax. Whatever is left is the tax, and it is split half CGST, half SGST.

Read the finished bill back and it holds together: taxable value 2,700 plus tax 198 equals the 2,898 the customer pays, and 2,898 is the 3,220 on the shelf less the 322 discount. Every invoice this app prints can be checked the same way.

CGST, SGST, IGST, UTGST

Which of the four appears on the bill is decided by geography, never by a person.

SituationWhat the customer is charged
Sale inside your own stateCGST + SGST, each half the rate. A 12% item becomes 6% + 6%
Sale to another stateIGST at the full rate. A 12% item is 12% IGST
Sale inside a union territory without its own legislatureCGST + UTGST, each half the rate
Any of the above with a cess itemCess is added on top of whichever of the three applies

The total does not change. Sell the same basket above to a customer in Karnataka and the tax is ₹198 of IGST instead of ₹99 + ₹99, and the customer still pays ₹2,898. Only the split changes — which matters for your returns, not for the customer's pocket.

Half of an odd rate cannot always be split evenly to the paisa. When it cannot, CGST takes the rounded half and SGST takes whatever is left, so the two together always equal the tax on the line exactly.

Where the sale takes place

The place of supply, and the order the app looks for it.

The app tries these in order and stops at the first one it finds. Every invoice records which one was used, so you can always see why a bill came out intra-state or inter-state.

Tax inside the price, or on top

One shop-wide choice that changes what the customer pays.

Inclusive — the price on the shelf is the price paid. The app works backwards to find how much of it was tax. A ₹1,050 shirt at 5% is ₹1,000 of value and ₹50 of tax; the customer pays ₹1,050.

Exclusive — the price on the shelf is before tax, and tax is added at the counter. A ₹1,000 shirt at 5% becomes ₹1,050 at the till.

The same goods priced both ways — shirts 5%, jeans 12%

Exclusive tags   2 × 1,000.00   1 × 1,000.003,000.00
  GST added at the till220.00
  Customer pays3,220.00
Same shop, same goods, same money. Only the number on the tag differs.
Which should a shop use

Whichever matches your price tags. If the tags show the price the customer pays, you are inclusive. Changing this setting does not re-price your products — it changes how the same number is read, so change it only with the accountant's agreement.

Discounts

Three kinds, all of which reduce taxable value.

  • Line discount — taken off one line only. Used mainly on supplier purchase entries.
  • Bill discount — a percentage or a rupee amount off the whole bill.
  • Loyalty points redeemed — treated exactly like a bill discount.

A bill discount is spread across the lines in proportion to their value before tax is worked out. This matters when the basket has more than one tax rate: ₹100 off a basket of 5% and 28% goods reduces the tax on both, in proportion, and never lands entirely on one rate.

When the shares don't add up

Proportional shares rounded to the paisa sometimes miss the discount by a paisa. Rather than lose it, the app puts the difference on the largest line.

₹25 off three lines of 50.00, 50.00 and 50.50
Line 1   25 × 50.00 ÷ 150.508.31
Line 2   25 × 50.00 ÷ 150.508.31
Line 3   25 × 50.50 ÷ 150.508.39
Adds up to25.01
a paisa too much, so the largest line gives it back
Line 3 corrected8.38
Discount given25.00

Two limits are enforced quietly: a discount can never exceed the value of the bill, and points redeemed plus discount together can never take the bill below zero.

Round-off

The last line before the total.

With round-off switched on, the payable amount is rounded to the nearest rupee and the adjustment is printed as its own line, so the invoice always adds up on paper.

Both directions
Bill comes to 599.40   round-off −0.40599.00
Bill comes to 599.60   round-off +0.40600.00

Round-off is a whole-rupee adjustment on the final amount only. It never touches the tax figures, and no other rounding is ever folded into this line.

The one-paisa question

A choice only your accountant can make, which is why the app leaves it to you.

When a price already includes tax and the numbers do not divide cleanly, two things cannot both be exact: the price on the shelf, and the tax figure as a straight percentage of the taxable value. They can differ by one paisa.

The clearest case is a ₹0.91 item at 28% GST plus 1% cess. Dividing by 1.29 gives ₹0.71 of value. From there:

SettingTaxableCGSTSGSTCessCustomer pays
Marked price is exact
tax is the remainder		0.710.100.090.010.91
Tax matches the rate
28% of 0.71, exactly		0.710.100.100.010.92

The first keeps your price tag honest — the customer pays what the tag says — at the cost of the GST shown sitting a paisa away from 28% of the taxable value. The second makes every tax figure reconcile against the rate, at the cost of billing a paisa more than the tag.

You choose, in Settings › Tax-Inclusive Price Rounding. The default is marked price is exact. On ordinary rate-and-price combinations the two settings give identical results — ₹1,050 at 5% is ₹1,000 and ₹50 either way — so most shops will never see the difference.

What the invoice remembers

Why an old invoice never changes when today's settings change.

At the moment a sale is saved, the app copies the facts that produced it onto the invoice itself — it does not look them up again later. Reprint a bill from six months ago and you get the bill as it was, not as today's settings would make it.

Frozen on every invoiceWhy it matters
Shop's legal name, address, GSTIN, stateMoving premises or changing the trade name does not rewrite old invoices
Place of supply and how it was decidedYou can always see why the bill was intra- or inter-state
Each line's rate, treatment, HSN, taxable value and every tax figureA rate change tomorrow leaves yesterday's bill alone
Document type — Tax Invoice or Bill of SupplyWhat the document legally was on the day
Customer name and GSTIN as recorded thenEditing a customer later does not alter their old bills

The rate book

Rates, cess, and why the bill's date matters.

The app ships with the standard slabs — 0%, 0.25%, 3%, 5%, 12%, 18% and 28% — each valid from 1 July 2017. A product points at one of them, and the rate is resolved against the date of the bill. That is what makes a reprint of an old invoice reproduce the old rate rather than the current one.

Cess, where a product carries one, is calculated separately from GST and shown as its own figure on the invoice. It is never merged into the GST amount.

A rate that is not in the standard list is accepted as long as the bill is dated on or after 1 July 2017 — so an unusual notified rate can be used without waiting for an app update.

Purchases from suppliers

The same calculator, pointed the other way.

When you record a supplier's invoice, the app runs the identical calculation — same rates, same place-of-supply logic, same rounding — with your shop as the buyer. That is how the input tax on a purchase and the output tax on a sale stay comparable in your reports.

One difference in practice: a supplier's invoice usually discounts individual lines rather than the whole bill, so line discounts are used there and are not spread across the other lines.

What this app does not do

Said plainly, so nothing is a surprise at filing time.

  • No e-invoicing or IRN. Invoices are not registered with the government portal and carry no IRN or signed QR code. If your turnover crosses the e-invoicing threshold, you need that separately.
  • No e-way bills. Nothing here generates or tracks them.
  • No GSTR filing. The app gives you the figures and the HSN summary; filing is done on the portal or by your accountant.
  • No reverse charge. Invoices print "Reverse Charge: No". Purchases attracting reverse charge need handling outside the app.
  • No digital signature. Invoices are signed by hand, which is why the signature block is printed by default. You can switch it off in Invoice Layout if you sign some other way.
  • No advice. The app applies the settings you give it. Which settings are right for your business is a question for your accountant.
